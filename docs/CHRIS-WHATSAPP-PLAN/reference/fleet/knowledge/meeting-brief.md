# Meeting brief

The two-minute brief before a meeting that matters, the follow-through after it, and the
shape of a notification to Patrick. Read in full before preparing a brief. Atlas's
meeting-prep behaviour covers his diary generally; this playbook covers only meetings
arising from your sourced prospects or relationships you established. A paused Atlas
does not transfer general meeting preparation or Patrick's diary to you.

## Before: the eight lines

For a meeting within your own relationship remit, produce a brief consumable in under two
minutes, in this order and nothing more:

WHO. Who this person is, in one line: role, firm, how we know them, relationship owner.
WHY. Patrick's commercial objective and why this person can help advance it, with evidence.
CURRENT CONTEXT. What matters to them now, from what they said or did, with its provenance
class (person said, verified, third party, inferred).
WHY THEY MAY CARE. Their expressed interest or a labelled hypothesis; unknown is acceptable.
LEAD WITH. At most three points, in order.
AVOID. Sensitivities, prior declines, anything internal only or confidential.
DESIRED OUTCOME. What would make this conversation worthwhile, and the next step to ask
for.
RELATIONSHIP HISTORY. Only the context useful for this meeting: last meaningful
interaction, the last ask we made, the last value we created, open commitments in either
direction.

Look everyone up before writing a word: the roster, the brain's meetings and CRM, your
notes, the profile and what they have actually said recently. A brief with nothing behind
it is not delivered as a template; it says in one line that nothing is held and what would
be worth capturing. Do not send a research essay. The brief is a file in your workspace
and one line in your report naming it; it interrupts nobody.

## After: follow-through

After a relevant meeting determine what changed, what was committed and by whom, by when,
the next step, whether another introduction now makes sense, what new ask and what new
offer were learned, and whether relationship strength moved. Then update your notes: the
person's record, the ask and offer ledger, the introduction's state, the commitment with
its owner and date. Patrick hears about it only where he needs awareness or action, and
then only OUTCOME, NEXT STEP, OWNER, DEADLINE.

Where a transcript exists, read it for commitments, asks, offers and possible connections
before writing any of the above. If you are ever a participant, listen, capture, identify
commitments, answer a factual question if invited, and never dominate.

## The notification shape

When something must reach Patrick, it is ONE ask.json card and its text follows this
order: WHO, WHY THIS ADVANCES YOUR BRIEF, EVIDENCE, THEIR LIKELY INTEREST, RECOMMENDATION,
DECISION. Offer independent candidate choices when batching is useful, and set an expiry
after which you act as if declined. If the last line has no concrete action for him, the
interruption is not justified: update the state and stay silent.
