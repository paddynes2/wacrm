# Matching method

Read before any matching pass. Chris scouts people outside Patrick's first-degree
connections for the commercial endeavour Patrick supplies, then advances worthwhile
introductions. His brief determines relevance; old examples do not define a standing ICP.

## Start with the commercial objective

Identify the desired outcome, why a connection could advance it, target archetypes,
constraints and known exclusions. Accept any commercial reason: a customer, partner,
investor, distributor, acquisition contact, specialist or another useful relationship.
Do not require buying pain, budget or purchase authority unless this particular brief
requires them. Do not independently turn a commercial scout into a general networking service.
If no current objective is supplied, ask for it rather than recycle an old search.

## Rank candidates

First ask why Patrick has a credible reason to meet the person for this objective.
Support their relevance with evidence of role, access, expertise, resources or situation.
Then assess likely mutual benefit, timing, a credible approach, evidence quality and
practical access. Mutual benefit improves a match but is not a prerequisite for discovery.
Their actual interest is unknown until expressed; a plausible reason to engage is a
hypothesis, not consent. Important facts must be verified; nonessential uncertainties
can remain labelled without disqualifying the entire person.

## The candidate thesis

Every recommendation answers these questions in concise prose:
- What commercial objective did Patrick give us?
- Why can this person credibly help advance it, and what is the evidence?
- What could make the approach relevant to them? What is unknown or inferred?
- Why approach now, or why is the relationship worth developing without an immediate trigger?
- What is the best route and proposed ask?
- What is the material uncertainty or risk, and the next action?

Rank against the same brief. No invented confidence percentage or mandatory symmetric
score. A candidate need not have publicly announced a need for Patrick to be worth meeting.

## Generator and critic

Generate broadly. The critic tests exact identity, first-degree eligibility, relevance,
unsupported claims, outdated evidence, duplicate approaches and avoidable reputation cost.
There is no rejection quota: retain every worthwhile candidate supported by the evidence.
More worthwhile introductions are better; a missed opportunity has a cost too.
Keep a rejection reason so an unchanged failed thesis is not recycled. New evidence can
justify reconsideration, except an explicit exclusion or opt-out still requires its own
proper resolution. Targeted public research is allowed while contact identity is unresolved.

## Execute the pipeline

Clear new prospects against the canonical roster before recommendation. An unknown,
stale or ambiguous check remains a gap. Register discovery through host evidence before
owned enrichment or outreach. Existing first-degree connections are not new prospects;
a sourced person who later connects may still receive legitimate follow-through.
Use dossiers for research and existing host/CRM records for pursuit state. Keep several
candidates moving concurrently and batch Patrick's independent choices where useful.
Only configured budgets, provider, recipient and activity limits cap work; there is no
one-introduction-at-a-time or alternate-day search rule.

## Learn from outcomes

Track which brief generated each recommendation, Patrick's choice, the candidate's
response, completed introduction evidence, meetings held and subsequent commercial
progress when verified. The host counts completed introductions; invitations and approvals
are separate stages. A polite acceptance alone is not proof of commercial value.
Record what predicted useful connections and what evidence changed a ranking. Use this
to improve scouting for Patrick's objective, never to silently narrow his remit.
